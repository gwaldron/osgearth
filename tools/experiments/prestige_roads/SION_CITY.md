# Sion city: urban roads, joined bridges, and railings

October 12, 2026. This separate dataset covers a **4 km east-west by 3 km north-south** metric rectangle,
centered on 7.365 E, 46.231 N. It includes central Sion, the Rhone crossings, the A9 at Chandoline,
and the Herens-Platta overpasses. The old small Sion experiment and the Boston, NYC, London, and Springfield
packages remain intact.

## Dataset and rendering

Four bounded OSM requests cover the rectangle plus a 500 m context margin. The source inventory has 3,421
selected road ways, including 56 bridge-tagged ways and 40 motorway links. These are source/context counts,
not counts of individual physical bridges. Complete source ways, node identity, layer/level tags, restrictions,
and marking evidence are retained. Tunnels are excluded from the height network and rendered road package.
The snapshot, retrieval URLs, hashes, merge policy, and OSM attribution are in `source/sion-city.osm.*`.

The urban compiler generates junction pavement, lane connections, turn arrows, stop bars, crossings,
curb bands, and gutters. The Swiss locale uses white center markings and yellow pedestrian crossings.
Mapped evidence and inferred layout/marking decisions are separate. Unresolved junction layouts are retained
as diagnostics; this is not a claim that every street has a complete lane-level reconstruction.

The completed package has **466 resolved junction layouts, 2,596 lane connections, 291 rendered mapped
crossing nodes, and 59 stop bars** inside the crop. Controls with ambiguous carriageway/direction attribution
remain in the evidence without a guessed stop bar. The **78 structure/raised-approach source surfaces** now
assemble into **49 deck sheets**, with **570,695 exported triangles including railings**. These counts are
compiler surfaces, not individual physical bridges.

Ground asphalt, paint, curbs, and gutters use separate Prestige material passes with R8 coverage, beginning
at LOD 15. Curbs here are terrain material bands, not vertical curb geometry. Structure decks, paint,
undersides, and edges are separate glTF geometry sharing the established material assets.
The bridge family tags remain in the source attribution; suspension/truss/arch superstructures and piers
are not generated by this slice.

The terrain remains the ordinary osgEarth regular grid. Absolute road centerlines and footprint masks drive
GradingLayer at one native LOD 16 with 257-square tiles and five-meter shoulders. Finer terrain reuses that
overlay; no-data falls through to ReadyMap. Ground beneath free spans is independent of deck height.
Short landing supports and inferred raised approaches use the same profiles, with recessed overlap support.

## Height and ownership assumptions

The network has 42,250 height variables before junction equivalence reduction. Relative OSM layer ordering
constrains crossings; it is never converted directly into a number of meters. Shared source nodes share
height variables. Crossings without mapped node connections remain disconnected.

Highways retain an 8% prototype grade cap. Sion permits 12% on primary/secondary/tertiary roads, 20% on local
streets, and 25% on service roads and cycleways. These are explicit feasibility limits for this terrain-derived
demo, not mapped grades, surveyed dimensions, or design-standard claims. The solve also limits cut to four
meters and balances terrain deviation against changes in longitudinal grade.

The illustrative slab is 0.8 m thick, tapering at ground connections. The road-crossing clearance target is
5.25 m. A Sion-specific 0.08 m interpolation allowance replaces the original 0.03 m after the composed-grid
audit found two edge samples approximately 1-2 cm below target. Railway clearance, water levels, hydraulic
clearance, and future inland-water grading are not solved.

Dense urban ground junctions do not all become horizontal equivalence patches: doing so propagates a flat
height through a connected street grid. Exact shared patches are restricted to structure connections in
this dataset. Ordinary ground profiles retain their slopes and are blended by the grading overlay.
Rounded pavement added by the urban compiler is explicitly associated with the nearest solved ground
centerline. That association is an approximation, not a new source connection.
Its grading influence is kept away from another profile's lower-road crossing receiver, including the
shoulder/filter margin. This corrected a small clearance regression introduced by rounded urban fill without
changing the accepted heights or pavement polygons. Disconnected fill pieces share 2,428 profile associations.

Short source-way splits at bridge landings require another distinction. Buffered pavement can overlap a
nearby connected bank road without passing above it. The compiler records bounded source-connected paths
and handles those local overlaps as landings. The actual centerline crossing and its local width stay
protected when the same road pair also crosses elsewhere. `split-approach-connections.json` records the
paths and source node/way identifiers; geometric proximity alone cannot create connectivity.

## Joined decks and railings

October 13, 2026. The previous model removed some duplicate pavement but extruded every source way
independently. That left internal slab faces at joins. Acute merges could also extend beyond the old
join-search radius, and overlaps smaller than 0.01 square meters were skipped by the height-solver inventory.
`deck_assembly.py` now follows shared OSM nodes through the full connected overlap, includes touching
degree-two continuations, and protects explicitly ordered crossings. It unions compatible footprints before
triangulating the slab and partitioning its asphalt and paint. The resulting 49 deck sheets contain no
unexpected positive-area overlap at the validation tolerance, and their fascia lies on exposed boundaries.

The continuous join field blends only source-connected neighbors within four meters of their interface.
It extends endpoint slopes through that small blend zone instead of turning source-way ends into flat pads.
The greatest sampled adjustment relative to a source profile is 0.1533 m, recorded per assembly. Original
solved centerline heights and grading inputs are unchanged. A network that crosses itself remains separate
in XY where necessary, with matching join fields and omitted internal fascia at its connected sheet boundaries.
A transient Springfield check exercises this case at 38 interface samples, agreeing to numerical precision;
its published dataset was not rebuilt in this pass.

One additional correction is explicit: ways 37429901 and 1230363975 are unconnected neighboring decks whose
lane-based default widths overlap at their edges. The compiler omits the ambiguous **28.950 m2** strip from
both decks while retaining their center corridors, source tags, and separate connectivity. This rule applies
only to inferred widths and never trims a declared ordered crossing. `deck-assemblies.json` records the
correction; the vector audit accounts for that entire footprint change independently.

There are **89 inferred railing runs**. They follow the assembled exterior edges with a 0.20 m inset and leave
mapped merge mouths, landings, crop boundaries, and explicitly unrailed surfaces open. Posts attach to the
actual exported triangles, rather than independently evaluating terrain. The galvanized-steel PBR material
uses two horizontal rails at 0.60 and 1.10 m, 1.15 m posts, and at most 2 m post spacing. These are illustrative
dimensions. Dedicated mapped barrier ways, traffic containment design, bridge-family superstructures, and
piers remain future work. The regular terrain grid, grading layer, imagery configuration, and indexed ground
package are unchanged.

New review outputs:

* `deck-surfaces.geojson`: merged pavement and marking polygons, with assembly IDs and source-way references.
* `structure-surfaces.geojson`: per-way evidence and material ownership, now including assembly IDs.
* `bridge-railings.geojson`: XYZ deck attachment paths, dimensions, and explicit inference attribution.
* `deck-assemblies.json`, `mesh-audit.json`: assembly membership, joins, width correction, and mesh ranges.
* `joined-export-validation.json`, `joined-vector-validation.json`: decoded mesh and vector comparisons.
* `grading-joined-query.csv`, `grading-joined-samples.csv`: fresh native elevation-pool probes at final boundaries.
* `merge.earth`, `railings.earth`: Google-imagery close views using the retained indexed ground layers.
* `validation-before-deck-assembly/`: the superseded export and terrain summaries.

The unchanged 0.25 mm float-render boundary tolerance passes for pavement and paint. Railing bases agree
with the decoded deck within 0.034 mm. Roundoff-size Boolean slivers are discarded before slab-edge
classification, while the original 1e-5 m2 area-conservation guard remains enforced. The original Boston
bridge still exports byte-identical glTF and binary files. Transient regressions cover acute merges,
touching continuations, stacked crossings, self-crossing networks, inferred-width separation, explicit

## Width transitions at bridge splits

October 13, 2026, follow-up to the eastern structure screenshot. The earlier exterior-boundary checks
missed a layout defect: the 7.4 m deck of way 906386786 meets two 4 m branches, ways 301438406 and
906386787, at shared node 8416737480. Both branch centerlines start at the same point. Unioning their
constant-width buffers briefly pinched the pavement and left square shoulders. The railings correctly
followed those incorrect shoulders, producing the two transverse sections in the screenshot.

`deck_transitions.py` now adds a local, inferred width transition before slab triangulation and railing
extraction. It connects nearby road cross sections at shallow forks and through-road width changes.
The extent is four times the full width difference, with an 8 m starting length, capped at 45% of each
incident way's length. These are bounded rendering assumptions, not surveyed dimensions or engineering
design criteria. Sharp junctions, connections to another sheet or terrain, and existing holes are excluded.
An unrelated receiver blocks the addition unless an existing ordered crossing and a sampled clearance
check justify the separation. Nanometer-scale redundant polygon vertices are removed before refinement;
the original area-conservation and 0.25 mm rendered-boundary checks remain in force.

The eastern split adds **23.089 m2** of deck. Two other Sion joins use the same rule, adding **44.037 m2**
and **6.229 m2**, for **73.355 m2** total. No original pavement or paint is removed by this correction.
`deck-transitions.geojson` contains only these added areas, their source ways, widths, node IDs, and
explicit inference attribution. `deck-assemblies.json` records the transition decisions. The per-way
source evidence, solved centerline elevations, ground grading, indexed ground data, and Google imagery
configuration are retained.

The rebuilt package still contains 49 assemblies and 89 railing runs, now with 570,064 triangles. The
independent decoded-mesh audit includes the added areas and expanded crossing footprints; it confirms
paint coverage, no internal fascia, and railing attachment within 0.034 mm. Fresh native elevation-pool
sampling checks **2,361 points**, retaining a minimum **5.291 m** underside clearance against the
5.25 m prototype target. No unexpected positive-area overlap occurs between Sion's deck sheets.

Transient regressions reproduce both a fork and a reversed merge, test the disappearance of transverse
railings at the old cap, and reject unrelated receivers, insufficient clearance, island filling, and sharp
junction shortcuts. The original Boston bridge remains byte-identical. Springfield's 38 cross-sheet

The `merge.earth` home camera now opens close to the corrected split. Matching NVGL/ICO captures are in

## Equal-width joins and the retained fat-merge policy

The second screenshot, located at approximately 7.37329 E, 46.23801 N, exposed a separate case near
nodes 8416737470 and 1139545868. The incoming and outgoing 4 m roads used differently oriented flat end
caps. Their union retained a wedge about 2.7 cm wide that reached the shared node. Offsetting the railing
inward widened this real geometric gap into the conspicuous spur across the roadway. The preceding
width-only correction did not run on this equal-width join.

The compiler now retains the user-approved **fat-merge policy** for both equal and unequal widths:
construct a shared cross-section envelope, fill local inside corners with a rolling circular offset, then
derive the slab and railings from that completed footprint. Corner rounding spans short source segments
instead of treating each vertex independently. It adds whole patches inside the bounded join region,
preserves source pavement, and retains the existing island, receiver, crossing-order, and clearance guards.
No renderer-specific workaround or Sion coordinate special case is involved.

Six transitions apply in the current Sion build, adding 94.240 m2 relative to the original source union;
one further candidate is explicitly deferred because it reaches an unrelated receiver. The reported ramp
gets 14.481 m2 of shared deck, and the opposite ramp's former short ledge now becomes a rounded corner.
The package has 49 assemblies, 89 railing runs, and 568,609 triangles. The exact reported source ways now
have a transient regression: the 1.95 m lane-core disk is covered and the 1.75 m core contains no railing.
Existing geometry regressions, Boston's byte-identical export, and Springfield's 38 interface checks pass.

The decoded-mesh check passes for all assemblies, paint footprints, fascia, and railing attachment.
All 44 profile-based crossing checks pass, with minimum clearance 5.319 m. **The new full terrain recheck
is incomplete:** Windows rejected outbound socket access to ReadyMap, so the base elevation layer could
not initialize. Of 2,440 current probe positions, 2,336 exactly match retained native samples with unchanged
grading inputs; their minimum clearance is 5.291 m. The other 104 positions remain unverified against the
composed terrain. Current validation JSON files explicitly record that status instead of carrying forward
the preceding complete-check claim. The original ground data, grading, and imagery configuration are unchanged.

`merge.earth` now opens this reported pair of joins, retaining the prior split camera as another preset.
Before/after geometry captures are in `captures-ramp-join`; the after capture has incomplete base terrain
and imagery due to the network failure. Previous validation and complete native sample files are preserved
in `validation-before-ramp-joins`. `grading-retained-samples.csv` contains only the verified matching subset;
the current `grading-joined-query.csv` and expected-height array are ready for a fresh native run.

## Reproduction

With the existing experiment Python environment, run `interchange.py fetch --scene sion-city`, followed by
`prepare`. Sample `terrain-query.csv` against `base.earth` with the native elevation-pool helper to produce
`terrain-samples.csv`. Then run `solve`, `build`, `city_network.py vectors --scene sion-city`, and
`city_network.py compose --scene sion-city`. The source fetch preserves an existing snapshot. Re-run the
independent mesh and composed-terrain checks before presenting a regenerated package.
